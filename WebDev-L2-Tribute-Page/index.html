Algebra and Algorithm.

Muhammad ibn Musa Al-Khwarizmi

A pioneer of mathematics, astronomy, and geography, Al-Khwarizmi helped transform how people solve problems and understand numbers. His work on algebra and arithmetic influenced scientific learning across the Islamic world and Europe.

About — His life and biography

Muhammad ibn Musa Al-Khwarizmi was a Muslim mathematician, astronomer, and geographer who lived during the ninth century, in the Islamic Golden Age. He worked in Baghdad, an important center of learning in the Abbasid era, and was associated with the scholarly environment of the House of Wisdom.

Al-Khwarizmi wrote influential works on solving mathematical equations and calculating with Hindu-Arabic numerals. His writings helped make mathematical methods more systematic and practical. Although many details of his personal life remain unknown, his surviving works left a lasting mark on mathematics and scientific education.

  • c. 780 CE — Birth

    • His birth is generally estimated around this time. The exact date and place are uncertain.

  • Early ninth century — Scholarship in Baghdad

    • He worked during a period of major scholarly activity under the Abbasid Caliphate.

  • c. 820–830 CE — Algebra

    • He composed his influential treatise on calculation by completion and balancing, systematically explaining methods for solving equations.

  • Ninth century — Astronomy and geography

    • His other scholarly works addressed astronomical calculations, calendars, and geographical coordinates.

  • Twelfth century — His works spread into Europe

    • Latin translations helped transmit mathematical knowledge to European scholars.

  • Today — A continuing influence

    • Algebra is fundamental to mathematics, while algorithms are essential to computer programming.
